  \begin{abstract}
    The companion line note types the Yukawa line as $\LY = \wedge^{2}(\Eweak)$ under [H-Weak] of Q14 ([H-Spin] for the
    fermion sectors) and reads levels on the model operator of $\Cgen$.
    Four premises, supplied by no source:
    [H-Res], the model operator is $\Epi^{2}|_{\Cgen}$;
    [H-Sq], $\Hpi = \Ygen^{\dagger}\Ygen = \lamY^{2}\,\Epi^{2}|_{\Cgen}$;
    [H-Fac], a weak linking carrier whose invariant couplings have multiplicity at most one, so that the results on
    the generation block $\Ygen$ reach $\Ypi$;
    [H-Grad], the $\Jthree$ grading of each generation carrier.
    The polar decomposition $\Ygen = \Upi\,\Hpi^{1/2}$ is linear algebra; under [H-Res] and [H-Sq],
    $\Spec(\Hpi) = \lamY^{2}\{1, \tfrac12 + u, \tfrac12 - u\}$, and the unitary $\Upi$, the chiral orientation and
    mixing, is invisible to the squared residue.
    Under [H-Grad], $[\Upi]$ is non-trivial at first order iff its polar generator $\Opol$ has a transverse part.
    The projection $\Aodd$ of the $\mathfrak{sl}_{2}$ lift onto its $\JPi$-odd anti-hermitian part vanishes, a
    selection no source justifies, and is not the polar generator.
    The polar factor of $\Sym^{2}g$ (real cascade step $g$, not a Yukawa morphism) has a non-zero external entry.
    Whether Yukawa morphisms with polar factor $\Upi$ exist is open.
    On the orientation-compatible branch of Q14, an input, the real diagonal split is a Lorentz-chirality
    imbalance, not the $V{-}A$ structure.
    Under [H-Res], [H-Sq] and the dictionary gate $u = 1/10$, the levels split at order one, not as the
    charged-fermion hierarchy.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Position and purpose}
    \label{sec:position}

    The line note~\cite{Beau2026pyl} types the coupling line conditionally.
    It types it as the determinant line $\LY = \wedge^{2}(\Eweak)$ of the distinct rank-two weak factor $\Eweak$
    of~\cite{Beau2026q14}, which carries the abelian weight only because the structure group of $\Eweak$ is $\UU(2)$,
    and it reads generation levels on the model operator $\diag(1, \tfrac12 + u, \tfrac12 - u)$ of $\Cgen$, as levels of
    $\Epi^{2}|_{\Cgen}$ under the premise [H-Res] of Section~\ref{sec:morphism}.
    The factor $\Eweak$ and the Lorentz reading of the spinor factor $\Sbundle$ are two hypotheses
    of~\cite{Beau2026q14}, [H-Weak] and [H-Spin], supplied by no source; under them the left fermions lie in
    $P_{L}\Sbundle \otimes \Eweak \otimes \LY^{k}$ and the right fermions in $P_{R}\Sbundle \otimes \LY^{m}$.
    The mass matrix is a distinct object: the projected Yukawa morphism $\Ypi$ between these sectors, whose norm, the
    sign of $u$, and the mixing phase remain open.

    This note analyses that morphism, and the analysis begins with a negative result.
    The squared projective residue is a hermitian object on a single Lorentz chirality, while $\Ygen$ ties the two
    chiralities; the chiral connecting datum is therefore not visible to $\Epi^{2}$.
    Concretely, under [H-Res] and [H-Sq] (Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:identification} and~\ref{hyp:square}),
    $\Epi^{2}|_{\Cgen}$ determines the hermitian square $\Hpi = \Ygen^{\dagger}\Ygen$, hence the squared Yukawa levels,
    but leaves $\Ygen$ free up to a unitary chiral polar factor.
    The purpose of the note is to make this precise, to extract exactly what is nonetheless fixed --- the
    spectrum of $\Ygen^{\dagger}\Ygen$ --- and to delimit what is not.
    Two kinds of statements are kept apart.
    The algebraic results use only the generation block $\Ygen : \GL \to \GR$ of the full morphism $\Ypi$, positive
    hermitian squares and, for the class results, the $\Jthree$ grading of the two generation carriers, taken as the
    premise [H-Grad] (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:grading}); the spinor, weak and line factors are spectators of the block.
    The other premises are named: [H-Res] and [H-Sq] for the reading of the model operator as the
    Yukawa square, [H-Fac] (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:factorisation}), made of two separate premises, for the transfer from
    the block to the full coupling, and the branch input of the Lorentz chirality (Section~\ref{sec:morphism}).
    Each statement is tagged below by the premises it uses.
    The discipline of the projective-residue reduction is kept throughout: $\Epi = -\Pi_{S} D (1 - P) D
    \Pi_{S}^{*} = -M^{\dagger} M$ is negative semi-definite, with the split $u$ carried by the transported
    chiral defect~\cite{Beau2026prs}, so a semi-definite operator must not be promoted by hand to an
    oriented chiral morphism.

  \section{The Yukawa operator as a chiral morphism}
    \label{sec:morphism}

    Under the hypotheses [H-Spin] and [H-Weak] of~\cite{Beau2026q14}, the left and right fermion sectors of the line
    note~\cite{Beau2026pyl} are
    \[
      \mathcal{F}_{L} = P_{L}\Sbundle \otimes \Eweak \otimes \LY^{k},
      \qquad
      \mathcal{F}_{R} = P_{R}\Sbundle \otimes \LY^{m},
      \qquad
      \LY = \wedge^{2}(\Eweak),
    \]
    each tensored with the generation factor $\Cgen$, on which the gauge group acts trivially (Theorem~5.2
    of~\cite{Beau2026q14}).
    The projected Yukawa morphism $\Ypi$ is the coupling between these sectors.
    The Lorentz-invariant pairing of a left and a right Weyl sector is sesquilinear: it pairs the conjugate of the
    $(\tfrac12, 0)$ module $P_{L}\Sbundle$, a $(0, \tfrac12)$ module, with the $(0, \tfrac12)$ module $P_{R}\Sbundle$,
    and the
    product $(0, \tfrac12) \otimes (0, \tfrac12) = (0, 0) \oplus (0, 1)$ contains the Lorentz scalar once.
    The Lorentz factor is therefore not an obstruction to the coupling; the weak factor is, as set out under
    [H-Fac] below.
    Write $G = \mathrm{Spin}(3,1) \times \UU(2)$ for the structure group of the bundles: the spin group of [H-Spin]
    acting on the spinor factors and $\UU(2)$ of [H-Weak] acting on $\Eweak$ and, through the determinant, on $\LY$
    (colour factors omitted).
    The group $G$ acts trivially on the generation factor.
    The full coupling is a $G$-invariant element of $\overline{\mathcal{F}_{L} \otimes \Cgen} \otimes
    (\mathcal{F}_{R} \otimes \Cgen) \otimes K$, with $K$ the weak linking carrier of [H-Fac] below.
    Its component on the generation factor, the generation block, is a sesquilinear form on $\GL \times \GR$,
    identified with the linear map
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:yukawa-morphism}
      \Ygen : \GL \longrightarrow \GR
    \end{equation}
    by $B(c_{L}, c_{R}) = \langle \Ygen c_{L}, c_{R} \rangle$, from the left generation carrier $\GL$ to the right
    generation
    carrier $\GR$.
    Both are copies of $\Cgen = \Sym^{2}(V_{\mathrm{gen}})$, and their gradings are the premise [H-Grad] below.
    The block $\Ygen$ is the full coupling $\Ypi$ only under the premise [H-Fac] below.

    The mass matrix is the value of the morphism $\Ypi$ on the locked configuration, whose generation block is
    $\Ygen$ under [H-Fac], and the two physically invariant
    hermitian objects are the left and right squares $\Ygen^{\dagger}\Ygen$ on $\GL$ and
    $\Ygen\,\Ygen^{\dagger}$ on $\GR$.
    The squared projective residue $\Epi^{2}$ is a hermitian operator on a single chirality.
    Under [H-Res] and [H-Sq] it is read as the left square up to the norm, and it cannot by itself carry
    the chiral connecting map between the two squares.
    This is the structural origin of the polar ambiguity below.

    The chiralities in this paper are Lorentz chiralities of the spinor factor under [H-Spin].
    They are not the electroweak chirality of $V{-}A$ selection, which is the representation content of [H-Weak] and
    which this note neither uses nor derives.

    In this paper $\JPi$ denotes the internal antilinear parity of the generation copy, an operator on $\Cgen$ built
    from the Born--Infeld datum ($HK = \varepsilon \circ \mathrm{conj}$ lifted to $\Sym^{2}$, Section~6
    of~\cite{Beau2026q14}); it does not use [H-Spin].
    The spinor-level parity that exchanges the chiral subbundles, used in~\cite{Beau2026ebj} and in Theorem~3.7
    of~\cite{Beau2026q14} under [H-Spin] and the Born--Infeld datum, is called the spinor-level Born--Infeld parity
    and is not denoted $\JPi$.

    \begin{hypothesis}[{[H-Res]}: the model operator on $\Cgen$ is the restriction of $\Epi^{2}$; not supplied]
      \label{hyp:identification}
      The model operator $\diag(1, \tfrac12 + u, \tfrac12 - u)$ on $\Cgen = \Sym^{2}(V_{\mathrm{gen}})$, with
      $V_{\mathrm{gen}}$ the distinct supplied generation doublet of~\cite{Beau2026q14} and $v = 0$, is the
      restriction to $\Cgen$ of the square $\Epi^{2}$ of the projective residue $\Epi$ of the projected Dirac
      operator, which exists under [H-Spin].
    \end{hypothesis}

    In~\cite{Beau2026q14} (Section~6) these operators are model operators on $\Cgen$, and their reading as
    restrictions of $\Epi^{2}$ is an identification that is not supplied.
    The Schur reduction~\cite{Beau2026prs} takes the model operator as a normal form on $\Cgen$ without deriving the
    identification.
    [H-Res] is a missing identification, not a refutation.
    It concerns the identity of the model operator and nothing else.
    Every statement that reads the model operator as $\Epi^{2}|_{\Cgen}$ is tagged [H-Res].

    \begin{hypothesis}[{[H-Sq]}: the Yukawa square is the residue square; not supplied]
      \label{hyp:square}
      With $\lamY > 0$ a normalisation, the left Yukawa square of the generation block is
      $\Hpi := \Ygen^{\dagger}\Ygen = \lamY^{2}\,\Epi^{2}|_{\Cgen}$.
    \end{hypothesis}

    [H-Sq] is a dictionary between the Yukawa square and the residue square.
    It is a separate premise from [H-Res] and is used together with it: [H-Res] fixes what $\Epi^{2}|_{\Cgen}$ is,
    [H-Sq] equates it with the Yukawa square.
    No source derives either.
    The level operator itself, $\diag(1, \tfrac12 + u, \tfrac12 - u)$, and the linear algebra built on it do not
    depend on them.

    \begin{hypothesis}[{[H-Fac]}: a weak linking carrier and multiplicity at most one; not supplied]
      \label{hyp:factorisation}
      Let $K$ be a finite-dimensional complex $\UU(2)$-module with trivial Lorentz action, the weak linking carrier,
      and let $I_{K} = (\overline{\mathcal{F}_{L}} \otimes \mathcal{F}_{R} \otimes K)^{G}$ be the space of
      $G$-invariant couplings of the two sectors, the generation factor omitted.
      [H-Fac] consists of two separate premises.
      \begin{enumerate}
        \item[\emph{(F1)}] \emph{Existence.} There is such a $K$ with $I_{K} \neq 0$.
        \item[\emph{(F2)}] \emph{Multiplicity at most one.} $\dim I_{K} \leq 1$.
      \end{enumerate}
      Under (F1) and (F2), $I_{K} = \mathbb{C}\,\Phi$ for one fixed invariant $\Phi$, and the full coupling is
      $\Phi \otimes \Ygen$ with $\Ygen \in \mathrm{Hom}(\GL, \GR)$ the generation block.
      I write [H-Fac1] for (F1) and [H-Fac2] for (F2), and [H-Fac] for their conjunction.
    \end{hypothesis}

    The two premises are independent: a carrier $K$ containing the required doublet twice satisfies (F1) and not (F2),
    and $K = 0$ satisfies (F2) and not (F1).
    Supplying one does not prove the other.
    The following representation theory, derived here and not stated by any source, fixes their content.
    Since $G$ acts trivially on $\Cgen$, the invariants of $\overline{\mathcal{F}_{L} \otimes \GL} \otimes
    (\mathcal{F}_{R} \otimes \GR) \otimes K$ are $I_{K} \otimes (\overline{\GL} \otimes \GR) = I_{K} \otimes
    \mathrm{Hom}(\GL, \GR)$ with no hypothesis, so a coupling is a sum $\sum_{i} \Phi_{i} \otimes Y_{i}$ over a basis
    $(\Phi_{i})$ of $I_{K}$.
    The Lorentz factor of $\overline{\mathcal{F}_{L}} \otimes \mathcal{F}_{R}$ is $(0, 0) \oplus (0, 1)$, and for a
    Lorentz-trivial $K$ only the scalar contributes.
    The weak factor of $\overline{\mathcal{F}_{L}} \otimes \mathcal{F}_{R}$ is
    $\overline{\Eweak \otimes \LY^{k}} \otimes \LY^{m} \cong \Eweak \otimes \LY^{m-k-1}$, since
    $\overline{\Eweak} \cong \Eweak \otimes \LY^{-1}$ for $\UU(2)$.
    It is an irreducible doublet, and the invariants of an irreducible module $W$ tensored with $K$ number the
    multiplicity of $W^{*}$ in $K$.
    Hence
    \[
      \dim I_{K} = \text{multiplicity of } \Eweak \otimes \LY^{\,k-m} \text{ in } K .
    \]
    So (F1) requires $K$ to contain the doublet $\Eweak \otimes \LY^{k-m}$, and (F2) requires it to occur at most
    once.
    A $\UU(2)$-character $K$ gives $I_{K} = 0$ for every $k$, $m$.
    In a convention where the coupling is a linear intertwiner $\mathcal{F}_{L} \otimes K \to \mathcal{F}_{R}$, the
    Lorentz part of $K$ must contain the vector $(\tfrac12, \tfrac12)$ and the weak twist becomes
    $\Eweak \otimes \LY^{m-k-1}$; both conventions require a $\UU(2)$-doublet component in $K$.
    The convention-independent element is therefore on the weak side: a carrier $K$ with a doublet component, the
    Higgs-type role, linking the weak doublet of $\mathcal{F}_{L}$ to $\mathcal{F}_{R}$.
    The right fermion $P_{R}\Sbundle \otimes \LY^{m}$ has no factor $\Eweak$:
    $\UU(2)$ acts on it through the character $\det^{m}$, so it is a weak singlet
    carrying the hypercharge twist $\LY^{m}$.
    The corpus supplies none of the three objects involved: $\Eweak$ is not constructed (Hypothesis~2.3
    of~\cite{Beau2026q14}), nor is $K$, nor the invariant $\Phi$.
    These are missing elements of the corpus.
    The conditions of the premise are the doublet component of $K$ for (F1) and its multiplicity at most one for (F2).
    Nothing proves that $K$ cannot be supplied, so this is a missing element, not a refutation.
    Without (F2) and with (F1), the full coupling is a sum of several generation blocks and the polar data of the
    full operator are not those of a single block.
    The results below are stated for the generation block.
    They transfer to the full operator only under [H-Fac]; without it, the polar decomposition, the rephasing class
    and the transverse criterion are statements about the block.

    \begin{hypothesis}[{[H-Grad]}: each generation carrier has its own $\Jthree$ grading; not supplied]

  \section{The squared Yukawa observable}
    \label{sec:squared}

    \begin{proposition}[Squared Yukawa observable]
      \label{prop:squared-yukawa}
      Let $\Ygen : \GL \to \GR$ be the generation block of the projected Yukawa morphism, and assume [H-Res] and [H-Sq]
      (Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:identification} and~\ref{hyp:square}).
      Then the generation-level observable determined by the Schur-residue sector is the positive hermitian
      operator
      \[
        \Hpi := \Ygen^{\dagger}\Ygen
      \]
      on the left generation carrier.
      On the gauge-singlet triplet $\Cgen$, its dimensionless spectral part is fixed, up to the overall
      Yukawa norm $\lamY$, by
      \[
        \Hpi\big|_{\Cgen} = \lamY^{2}\,\diag\!\left(1,\ \tfrac12 + u,\ \tfrac12 - u\right) ,
      \]
      so that $\Spec\big(\Ygen^{\dagger}\Ygen\big) = \lamY^{2}\,\{1,\ \tfrac12 + u,\ \tfrac12 - u\}$.
      Thus $\Epi^{2}|_{\Cgen}$ determines the squared Yukawa levels, not the Yukawa morphism itself.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      The Schur reduction gives $\Epi = -M^{\dagger} M$ negative semi-definite~\cite{Beau2026prs}, so $\Epi^{2}$ is
      positive semi-definite.
      By [H-Res], the model operator $\diag(1, \tfrac12 + u, \tfrac12 - u)$, the normal form of~\cite{Beau2026prs} with
      $v = 0$ whose even part $(C_{2} - \Jthree^{2})/C_{2} = \diag(1, \tfrac12, \tfrac12)$ is algebraic, is
      $\Epi^{2}|_{\Cgen}$.
      By [H-Sq], $\Hpi = \Ygen^{\dagger}\Ygen = \lamY^{2}\,\Epi^{2}|_{\Cgen}$ with $\lamY$ the undetermined overall
      norm,
      which gives $\Hpi|_{\Cgen} = \lamY^{2}\,\diag(1, \tfrac12 + u, \tfrac12 - u)$.
      The eigenvalues are the diagonal entries, so the spectrum is as stated, and a hermitian square fixes a
      hermitian operator, not the underlying morphism, which is the content of Section~\ref{sec:polar}.
    \end{proof}

    Premises: [H-Res] and [H-Sq] for the identification, the generation block, and positive hermitian squares;
    the spinor, weak and line factors are spectators of the block, and [H-Fac] is needed to read $\Hpi$ as the left
    square of the full operator.

    The positive root of $\Epi^{2}$ on its domain is $-\Epi$ itself, since $\Epi$ is negative semi-definite.
    [H-Res] concerns $\Epi^{2}$ only: it supplies no restriction of $\Epi$ to $\Cgen$, so $-\Epi|_{\Cgen}$ is not read
    as the positive root $\Hpi^{1/2}/\lamY$ of the model operator.
    No oriented chiral information has been used, in keeping with the reduction discipline.

  \section{Polar decomposition and the undetermined chiral phase}
    \label{sec:polar}

    \begin{proposition}[Chiral polar ambiguity]
      \label{prop:polar-ambiguity}
      The operator $\Hpi = \Ygen^{\dagger}\Ygen$ determines $\Ygen$ only up to a polar factor:
      \[
        \Ygen = \Upi\,\Hpi^{1/2} ,
      \]
      where
      \[
        \Upi : \GL \longrightarrow \GR
      \]
      is a unitary chiral polar map on the support of $\Hpi$.
      Consequently, under [H-Res] and [H-Sq], the squared residue fixes the generation levels but not
      the complex mixing phase, nor the full Yukawa operator.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      For a positive hermitian $\Hpi$ with $\Hpi^{1/2}$ its positive square root, any $\Ygen$ with
      $\Ygen^{\dagger}\Ygen = \Hpi$ satisfies $\Ygen = \Upi\,\Hpi^{1/2}$ with $\Upi := \Ygen\,\Hpi^{-1/2}$ a
      partial isometry, unitary on the support of $\Hpi$, since $\Upi^{\dagger}\Upi = \Hpi^{-1/2}
      \Ygen^{\dagger}\Ygen\,\Hpi^{-1/2} = \Hpi^{-1/2}\Hpi\,\Hpi^{-1/2} = \mathbf{1}$ on that support.
      Conversely, for any such $\Upi$ the product $\Upi\,\Hpi^{1/2}$ has square $\Hpi^{1/2}\Upi^{\dagger}
      \Upi\,\Hpi^{1/2} = \Hpi$, so the solution set is exactly $\{\Upi\,\Hpi^{1/2}\}$.
      The map $\Upi$ acts into the right carrier $\GR$, and $\Hpi$ is invariant under $\Ygen \mapsto V\,\Ygen$
      for every unitary $V$ on $\GR$, $(V\Ygen)^{\dagger}(V\Ygen) = \Ygen^{\dagger} V^{\dagger} V\,\Ygen =
      \Ygen^{\dagger}\Ygen$, so distinct $\Upi$ give distinct $\Ygen$ with identical $\Hpi$.
      Hence $\Hpi$, equivalently (under [H-Res] and [H-Sq]) $\Epi^{2}|_{\Cgen}$, does not
      determine $\Ygen$: the generation-mixing
      content and the relative phases reside in $\Upi$, on which $\Epi^{2}$ has no hold.
    \end{proof}

    Premises: the generation block and positive hermitian squares only; the first assertion is linear algebra and
    uses none of [H-Res], [H-Sq], [H-Fac].
    [H-Res] and [H-Sq] enter only the reading of $\Hpi$ as $\Epi^{2}|_{\Cgen}$, and [H-Fac] the transfer to the full
    operator.

    \begin{remark}[The partial no-go, sharply]
      \label{rem:no-go}
      Proposition~\ref{prop:polar-ambiguity} is a partial no-go: $\Epi^{2}$, read as $\Hpi$ under
      [H-Res] and [H-Sq], does not fix $\Ygen$.
      What survives is exactly the spectral datum of Proposition~\ref{prop:squared-yukawa},
      $\Spec(\Ygen^{\dagger}\Ygen) = \lamY^{2}\{1, \tfrac12 + u, \tfrac12 - u\}$.
      The undetermined factor is not a numerical residue but a unitary chiral map, the carrier of the chiral
      orientation, the relative generation phases, and the mixing.
      The positive scale $\lamY$ is a separate normalisation of the hermitian square $\Hpi$, invisible to the
      dimensionless level operator $\Epi^{2}|_{\Cgen}$, and it is not carried by the unitary $\Upi$.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[The open datum in $u$ is the orientation branch, not the scalar sign]
      \label{rem:orientation}
      In an oriented $\Jthree$-labelled generation basis $(e_0, e_+, e_-)$ the level operator
      $\diag(1, \tfrac12 + u, \tfrac12 - u)$ already sees the relative sign of $u$.
      The operator $\Hpi$ therefore fixes the sign of $u$ only after such an oriented basis has been chosen
      (under [H-Res] and [H-Sq] for the reading as $\Hpi$).
      Without that chiral-orientation choice the exchange $e_+ \leftrightarrow e_-$ sends $u \mapsto -u$ and
      leaves the unordered squared spectrum unchanged.
      Thus the remaining datum is the physical orientation branch of the split, not an additional scalar
      visible to $\Epi^{2}$ alone.
    \end{remark}
    \begin{polargen}
      Let $\gamma$ be a real parameter and $\Ygen(\gamma) : \GL \to \GR$ a smooth family of invertible morphisms
      between fixed orthonormal frames, $\Ygen = \Upi\,\Hpi^{1/2}$ with $\Hpi^{1/2} > 0$.
      The polar generator is $\Opol = \Upi^{\dagger}\,\partial_{\gamma}\Upi$, an anti-hermitian operator.
      With $\Xi = \Upi^{\dagger}\,\partial_{\gamma}\Ygen$ one has
      $\Xi = \Opol\,\Hpi^{1/2} + \partial_{\gamma}\Hpi^{1/2}$, and subtracting the adjoint gives the Sylvester equation
      \[
        \Opol\,\Hpi^{1/2} + \Hpi^{1/2}\,\Opol = \Xi - \Xi^{\dagger}
        = \Upi^{\dagger}\,\partial_{\gamma}\Ygen - (\partial_{\gamma}\Ygen)^{\dagger}\,\Upi .
      \]
    \end{polargen}

    The operator $X \mapsto X\Hpi^{1/2} + \Hpi^{1/2}X$ has the eigenvalues $h_{i} + h_{j} > 0$, with $h_{i}$ the
    eigenvalues of $\Hpi^{1/2}$, so it is invertible and $\Opol$ is determined: in an eigenbasis of $\Hpi^{1/2}$,
    $\Opol_{ij} = (\Xi - \Xi^{\dagger})_{ij}/(h_{i} + h_{j})$.
    At $\Ygen(0) = \mathbf{1}$ it reduces to $\Opol(0) = \tfrac12\big(\partial_{\gamma}\Ygen(0) -
    \partial_{\gamma}\Ygen(0)^{\dagger}\big)$.
    The formula assumes a real parameter, fixed orthonormal frames on both carriers and $\Hpi^{1/2} > 0$.
    Singular supports ($\Hpi$ not invertible) and moving frames (frame transport, covariant derivative) need a separate
    treatment, and the formula is not applied there.
    The Sylvester equation, its unique solution and the identity at $\mathbf{1}$ are checked in exact arithmetic in
    \texttt{front\_polar\_generator.py} (Section~\ref{sec:repro}).

  \section{Diagonal CP-real branch}
    \label{sec:cp-real}

    The reduction~\cite{Beau2026prs} imposes $v = 0$ on the CP-even real sector, so that the off-diagonal mixing
    component $R_{\mathrm{mix}}$ of the model operator vanishes there.
    In the metaplectic step model of~\cite{Beau2026q14} (Section~6, Remark~6.4) no generator reaches $R_{\mathrm{mix}}$,
    whether real or complex.
    The diagonal CP-real branch is defined here by $\Upi = \mathbf{1}$; it is not implied by $v = 0$, which concerns
    $\Epi^{2}$.
    There the Yukawa block is, under [H-Res] and [H-Sq], the positive root itself,
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:cp-real}
      \Ygen^{\mathrm{CP}} = \Hpi^{1/2} = \lamY\,\diag\!\left(1,\ \sqrt{\tfrac12 + u},\ \sqrt{\tfrac12 - u}\right),
    \end{equation}
    real, diagonal in the generation basis, with no mixing.
    This is a choice of branch, not a derived fact: a non-trivial $\Upi$ --- a generation rotation or a
    relative phase --- leaves $\Hpi$ and the levels untouched while injecting off-diagonal and complex
    structure that $\Epi^{2}$ cannot register.
    The mixing and the CP phase therefore live entirely in $\Upi$, whose first-order content is the polar generator
    $\Opol$ defined in Section~\ref{sec:polar}; its transverse part has two blocks.
    The external block $e_{+} \leftrightarrow e_{-}$, that is the direction $R_{\mathrm{mix}}$ along which
    \cite{Beau2026prs} expands $\Epi^{2}$, has no matrix element in the image of $\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})$ in
    $\mathrm{End}(\Sym^{2} V_{\mathrm{gen}})$ (Remark~6.4 of~\cite{Beau2026q14}, a statement on matrix elements of the
    lift); this is not a statement on the entries of a polar factor.
    For the internal block $e_{0} \leftrightarrow e_{\pm}$, Remark~6.4 of~\cite{Beau2026q14} is silent, and
    Proposition~6.3 (i)--(ii) of~\cite{Beau2026q14} excludes it by hypothesis, for the oriented odd lift it considers,
    which annihilates the central level and preserves the outer doublet.
    The projection $\Aodd$ of the $\mathfrak{sl}_{2}$ lift onto the $\JPi$-odd anti-hermitian part, defined in
    Remark~\ref{rem:two-transverse-blocks}, vanishes identically for every complex coefficient
    (Proposition~\ref{prop:reality-structure}); this is a statement about $\Aodd$ as defined, and $\Aodd$ does
    not determine the polar factor (Section~\ref{sec:polar-class}).
    The $\JPi$-even anti-hermitian part of the lift has a non-zero internal entry, non-zero for real $p \neq q$, and
    it is the polar generator at the identity for a family with that tangent, so the image of
    $\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})$ does reach the internal block through that part.
    No physical exclusion of the internal block is claimed.

  \section{Polar class of the projected Yukawa operator}
    \label{sec:polar-class}

    The polar factor $\Upi$ of Section~\ref{sec:polar} is not a canonical observable before one quotients by
    the admissible chiral rephasings.
    The admissible rephasings of a carrier are the unitaries that preserve its $\Jthree$ grading, that is, the unitaries
    commuting with $\Jthree = \diag(0, 1, -1)$; the grading of each carrier is the premise [H-Grad]
    (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:grading}).
    The weights $0, +1, -1$ are distinct, so these are the diagonal unitaries.
    They are defined by the carrier, a copy of $\Cgen$ with its own grading, and not by $\Ygen$.
    The level operator $\Hpi$ is positive and commutes with $\Jthree$; for $0 < u < \tfrac12$ its spectrum
    $\lamY^{2}\{1, \tfrac12 + u, \tfrac12 - u\}$ (under [H-Res] and [H-Sq]) is distinct, so on the left carrier its
    eigenbasis is the weight basis.
    The right carrier carries the level operator $\Ygen\,\Ygen^{\dagger} = \Upi\,\Hpi\,\Upi^{\dagger}$, which has the
    same
    spectrum as $\Hpi$ as for any square matrix, and whose eigenbasis is the right weight basis rotated by $\Upi$.
    The class of $\Upi$ measures this misalignment of the right level eigenbasis with the right $\Jthree$ grading.
    The antiunitary exchange of the two carriers of~\cite{Beau2026prs} gives the same spectral reading and is not
    used.

    \begin{proposition}[Polar class]
      \label{prop:polar-class}
      Assume [H-Grad].
      Let $\Hpi = \Ygen^{\dagger}\Ygen$ be positive, commuting with $\Jthree$, with distinct eigenvalues, as
      $\lamY^{2}\,\diag(1, \tfrac12 + u, \tfrac12 - u)$ with $0 < u < \tfrac12$ is under [H-Res] and [H-Sq].
      Let $V_{L} \in \UU(1)^{3}$ act on $\GL$ and $V_{R} \in \UU(1)^{3}$ on $\GR$ as the diagonal rephasings of the
      weight bases, that is the unitaries commuting with $\Jthree$ on each carrier.
      Then $\Ygen \mapsto V_{R}\,\Ygen\,V_{L}^{-1}$, $\Hpi \mapsto V_{L}\,\Hpi\,V_{L}^{-1} = \Hpi$, and
      \[
        \Upi \longmapsto V_{R}\,\Upi\,V_{L}^{-1} ,
      \]
      so the projected Yukawa construction carries not a preferred matrix $\Upi$ but its rephasing class, the
      double quotient
      \[
        [\Upi] \in \UU(1)^{3}_{R} \,\backslash\, \UU(3) \,/\, \UU(1)^{3}_{L} .
      \]
      The class $[I]$ is the set of diagonal unitaries.
      Its rephasing invariants are the moduli $|(\Upi)_{ij}|$ together with the Jarlskog phase, written $\JCP$ to
      keep it distinct from the antilinear parity $\JPi$:
      \[
        \JCP = \Im\!\big( (\Upi)_{11}(\Upi)_{22}\,\overline{(\Upi)_{12}}\,\overline{(\Upi)_{21}} \big) .
      \]
      The common diagonal phase acts trivially on the double action, so the class space has dimension
      $9 - (2\cdot 3 - 1) = 4$, namely three mixing angles and one CP phase, and the differential of the moduli and
      $\JCP$ has rank four at generic points.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      The commutant of $\Jthree$, a diagonal matrix with the distinct entries $0, 1, -1$, consists of diagonal
      matrices, since $(W\Jthree - \Jthree W)_{ij} = W_{ij}({\Jthree}_{jj} - {\Jthree}_{ii})$.
      The groups $\UU(1)^{3}_{L}$ and $\UU(1)^{3}_{R}$ are therefore fixed by the gradings of the two carriers, with
      no reference to $\Ygen$ or to the eigenbasis of $\Ygen\,\Ygen^{\dagger}$.
      The positive operator $\Hpi$ commutes with $\Jthree$ and has distinct eigenvalues, so it is diagonal in the weight
      basis and commutes with $V_{L}$ and with $\Hpi^{-1/2}$.
      Hence $\Hpi' = V_{L}\,\Ygen^{\dagger}\Ygen\,V_{L}^{-1} = \Hpi$ and
      $\Upi' = V_{R}\,\Ygen\,V_{L}^{-1}\Hpi^{-1/2} = V_{R}\,\Upi\,V_{L}^{-1}$.
      The choice $V_{R} = \Upi$ would give $V_{R}^{-1}\,\Ygen = \Hpi^{1/2}$ and remove $\Upi$ entirely.
      But $\Upi$ commutes with $\Jthree$ only if it is diagonal, so it is not an admissible rephasing.
      Pinning $\GR$ by the eigenbasis of $\Ygen\,\Ygen^{\dagger}$ would be this choice, and the right group is not
      defined that way.
      Under rephasings $(\Upi)_{ij} \mapsto e^{i(a_{i} - b_{j})}(\Upi)_{ij}$, so the moduli are invariant and the
      quartet phase of $\JCP$ changes by $(a_{1} - b_{1}) + (a_{2} - b_{2}) - (a_{1} - b_{2}) - (a_{2} - b_{1}) = 0$.
      The orbit of $I$ is $\{V_{R}V_{L}^{-1}\}$, the diagonal unitaries.
      A rephasing fixing $\Upi$ satisfies $(\Upi)_{ij}(e^{ia_{i}} - e^{ib_{j}}) = 0$, so for a $\Upi$ with no vanishing
      entry, an open dense set, all $e^{ia_{i}}$ and $e^{ib_{j}}$ coincide: the stabiliser is the common phase.
      Orbits there have dimension $2\cdot 3 - 1 = 5$, and the dimension of $\UU(3)$ being nine, four physical parameters
      survive, three angles and one phase.
      The orbit dimension five, from the differential of the rephasing action, and the rank four of the differential of
      the moduli and $\JCP$ are computed in \texttt{front\_polar\_class\_rephasing.py}.
    \end{proof}

    Premises: the generation block, [H-Grad] for the $\Jthree$ grading of the two carriers, in particular of the right
    carrier, and a positive $\Hpi$ commuting with $\Jthree$ with distinct spectrum; [H-Res] and [H-Sq] enter only
    through the reading of that
    $\Hpi$ as $\lamY^{2}\diag(1, \tfrac12 + u, \tfrac12 - u)$, and [H-Fac] through the transfer to the full operator.
    The spinor, weak and line factors are spectators of the block.
    The distinctness of the spectrum makes the left group $\UU(1)^{3}_{L}$ equal to the commutant of $\Hpi$.
    At $u = 0$ the commutant of $\Hpi$ is larger than $\UU(1)^{3}_{L}$.
    The class defined here is then finer than the class under that commutant, so the statement is made for
    $0 < u < \tfrac12$.

    The next obstruction is therefore not the existence of $\Upi$, but the non-triviality of its class: whether
    the polar generator of a morphism family has a transverse part, giving $[\Upi] \neq [I]$, or only a representative
    diagonal-rephasing equivalent to the identity.

    \begin{proposition}[Polar non-triviality]
      \label{prop:polar-nontriviality}
      Let $\Upi(\gamma) = \exp(\gamma A)$ with $A$ anti-hermitian: the polar factor of a family whose polar generator
      is constant, $\Opol = A$ (for a general smooth family, $A = \Opol(0)$ is the tangent at $\gamma = 0$).
      The diagonal-rephasing orbit through the identity has tangent space the diagonal anti-hermitian
      matrices, so $[\Upi(\gamma)] \neq [I]$ at first order if and only if $A$ has a non-zero off-diagonal
      (transverse) part.
      The rephasing-invariant detectors may be taken as
      \[
        |(\Upi)_{ij}|^{2} = \gamma^{2}|A_{ij}|^{2} + O(\gamma^{3}), \qquad i \neq j ,
      \]
      or, equivalently for a positive branch of $\gamma$, $\partial_{\gamma}^{+}|(\Upi)_{ij}|_{\gamma = 0} =
      |A_{ij}|$, so the off-diagonal moduli detect precisely the transverse component of the
      generator; the Jarlskog invariant has leading non-vanishing order $\gamma^{3}$ and is proportional to
      $\Im(A_{12}A_{23}A_{31})$, independent of the diagonal phases.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      A curve $V_{R}(s)\,V_{L}(s)^{-1}$ through the identity in the rephasing group has derivative
      $i(D_{R} - D_{L})$ at $s = 0$, diagonal and anti-hermitian, and every diagonal anti-hermitian is so
      reached; hence the orbit tangent at $I$ is exactly the diagonal anti-hermitian subspace.
      For $\Upi(\gamma) = \exp(\gamma A)$ the off-diagonal entry is $(\Upi)_{ij} = \gamma A_{ij} + O(\gamma^{2})$
      so $|(\Upi)_{ij}|^{2} = \gamma^{2}|A_{ij}|^{2} + O(\gamma^{3})$, and the moduli detect precisely the
      transverse part of $A$.
      The quartet phase vanishes at orders $\gamma^{0}, \gamma^{1}, \gamma^{2}$ and its leading $\gamma^{3}$
      coefficient is proportional to $\Im(A_{12}A_{23}A_{31})$, the genuine three-generation datum, with no
      dependence on the diagonal phases.
    \end{proof}

    Premises: the generation block, and [H-Grad] for the diagonal-rephasing orbit tangent.
    The statement holds for every curve $\Upi(\gamma) = \exp(\gamma A)$ of unitaries with anti-hermitian $A$.
    For a morphism family the first-order content of the polar factor is $\Opol(0)$, fixed by the Sylvester equation
    above.
    The corpus supplies no family of Yukawa morphisms whose polar factor is $\Upi$, so the proposition is applied to no
    physical $\Upi$ here; [H-Fac] is needed to read the block class as that of the full operator.

    The criterion is first-order.
    A vanishing tangent component does not exclude an off-diagonal entry at higher order.
    For $\Upi(\gamma) = \exp(\gamma^{2}K)$ with $K$ anti-hermitian and off-diagonal, $\Opol(0) = 0$ and the off-diagonal
    entries are of order $\gamma^{2}$.
    For the polar factor of $\Sym^{2}g(\gamma)$ with $g = \exp(2\gamma E)\exp(\gamma F)$, the external entry
    $e_{+} \leftrightarrow e_{-}$ of $\Opol(0)$ vanishes while the external entry of the finite factor is
    $\gamma^{2}/4 + O(\gamma^{4})$.

    \paragraph{The projection \texorpdfstring{$\Aodd$}{A\_Pi odd} is not the polar generator.}
      Let $L(M)$ be the $\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})$ lift of Remark~\ref{rem:two-transverse-blocks}, and take a
      family with $\Ygen(0) = \mathbf{1}$ and $\partial_{\gamma}\Ygen(0) = L(M)$.
      The Sylvester equation gives $\Opol(0) = \tfrac12\big(L(M) - L(M)^{\dagger}\big)$, the $\JPi$-even
      anti-hermitian part of $L(M)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:reality-structure} (ii)), non-zero for instance for
      $p = 1$, $q = r = 0$.
      The projection $\Aodd$ is the anti-hermitian part of the $\JPi$-odd part of $L(M)$ and vanishes identically.
      Hence $\Aodd$ is not the polar generator of a family with that tangent: they are distinct objects.

      An exact real counterexample shows that a vanishing external tangent does not exclude a finite external entry.
      For $g = \exp(tE)\exp(sF) = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 1 + ts & t \\ s & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$
      with real $t, s$, the unitary polar factor of $g$ is
      \[
        u = \frac{1}{\sqrt{(2 + ts)^{2} + (t - s)^{2}}}
        \begin{pmatrix} 2 + ts & t - s \\ s - t & 2 + ts \end{pmatrix}.
      \]
      The normalised symmetric-square representation preserves adjoints, unitarity and positivity, so the unitary polar
      factor of $\Sym^{2}g$ is $\Sym^{2}u$.
      Its entry $(e_{+}, e_{-})$ is $(t - s)^{2}/\big((2 + ts)^{2} + (t - s)^{2}\big)$: it equals $1/5$ at
      $(t, s) = (1, 0)$ and $1/17$ at $(t, s) = (2, 1)$, an interior cascade step with $ts > 0$, where
      $u = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 4 & 1 \\ -1 & 4 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)/\sqrt{17}$ and the entries between
      $e_{0}$ and $e_{\pm}$ are $\pm 4\sqrt{2}/17$.
      A zero rotation occurs for $t = s$.
      For $(t, s) = (2\gamma, \gamma)$ the tangent at $0$ is $L\big(\tfrac12(E - F)\big)$, non-zero and $\JPi$-even,
      while the projection of $L(E)$ vanishes.
      The absence of an external entry in an infinitesimal generator therefore does not exclude one in the finite
      factor.
      The real factor has zero Jarlskog invariant: non-zero mixing moduli with real data do not establish CP violation.
      This is a mathematical distinction, not a contradiction of the vanishing $R_{\mathrm{mix}}$ coefficient of the
      Hermitian square~\cite{Beau2026prs} nor of the absence of a matrix element of the $\mathfrak{sl}_{2}$ lift
      along $R_{\mathrm{mix}}$~\cite{Beau2026q14}, which concern different objects.

      The square does not determine the polar data.
      For a fixed $D > 0$, $Y_{0} = D$ and $Y_{1} = VD$ have the same square $D^{2}$ for every unitary $V$, and
      $V = \Sym^{2}u$ gives an exact countermodel.
      This is a non-identifiability from the square, not the impossibility of an additional physical construction.

      The bridge is a missing element, not a refutation.
      The corpus supplies no family of Yukawa morphisms $\Ygen(\gamma) : \GL \to \GR$ whose polar factor is $\Upi$.
      A metaplectic step $g$ is not a Yukawa morphism and its square is not automatically the prescribed $\Hpi$.
      Reading the polar factor of $\Sym^{2}g$ as $\Upi$ would need an explicit bridge: normalisation, carrier
      identification, Hermitian metrics, parameter and frame transport, and the relative data of the two sectors.
      The $\JPi$-odd part is not replaced by the $\JPi$-even part by decree.
      The checks are in \texttt{front\_polar\_generator.py} (Section~\ref{sec:repro}).

    \begin{corollary}[Vanishing of $\Aodd$ on the real cascade]
      \label{cor:real-cascade-collapse}
      On the real cascade of the model the off-diagonal mixing channel $R_{\mathrm{mix}}$ of $\Epi^{2}$ is absent,
      $v = 0$, the transported chiral defect being the real diagonal split~\cite{Beau2026prs}; this concerns
      $\Epi^{2}$ and not the class $[\Upi]$.
      On the branch input the left-admissibility condition reads $\pi_{LL} = 0$, so the split is carried by the
      imbalance $\pi_{RR}$ of the eliminated block: after restriction to $\Cgen$, under [H-Res],
      $u \propto \pi_{RR} - \pi_{LL}$ up to the Weyl transport, refined by the transported defect
      $\Delta_{\chi} = \pi_{LL} - \tau\,\overline{\pi_{RR}}\,\tau^{-1}$ of~\cite{Beau2026prs}, with $\tau$ the unitary
      intertwiner of the chirality exchange.
      This is a statement on Lorentz chirality under [H-Spin] and the branch input, not the $V{-}A$ structure of
      [H-Weak]; the wording ``spontaneous $V{-}A$ choice'' for the sign of $u$ in~\cite{Beau2026prs} is not adopted.
      In a covariant moving family, with a covariant moving spinorial embedding and a Dirac operator invariant under
      the spinor-level Born--Infeld parity of~\cite{Beau2026ebj}, the
      first-order coefficient of the external component $R_{\mathrm{mix}}$ of $\Epi^{2}$ vanishes by antiunitary
      parity, and so does every odd-order coefficient; mixing at even orders is not excluded, and the internal entries
      $e_{0} \leftrightarrow e_{\pm}$ are not constrained by that parity argument~\cite{Beau2026ebj}.
      The projection $\Aodd$ of the $\mathfrak{sl}_{2}$ lift, defined in Remark~\ref{rem:two-transverse-blocks},
      vanishes for every complex coefficient, the real cascade data included
      (Proposition~\ref{prop:reality-structure}).
      $\Aodd$ is not the polar generator of a family of morphisms (paragraph above): it does not determine $\Upi$,
      and no statement on $[\Upi]$ follows from $\Aodd = 0$.
      For a family $\Ygen(\gamma)$ with $\Ygen(0) = 1$ and tangent the $\Sym^{2}$ lift $L(M)$ at the identity, the polar
      generator $\Opol(0)$ is non-zero and $\JPi$-even; the cascade step $g$ is not at the identity, and the exact
      counterexample concerns the finite unitary polar factor of $\Sym^{2}g$ (not a Yukawa morphism), whose external
      entry is $1/17$ at $(t, s) = (2, 1)$, with zero Jarlskog invariant.
      The external block $R_{\mathrm{mix}}$ has no matrix element in the image of $\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})$
      (Remark~6.4 of~\cite{Beau2026q14}, a statement on matrix elements of the lift); this is not a statement on the
      entries of a polar factor.
      The internal block is reached by the $\JPi$-even anti-hermitian part of the lift, which is not part of
      $\Aodd$.
      Whether a family of Yukawa morphisms with polar factor $\Upi$ exists, and which polar class $[\Upi]$ it has, is
      open.
      A resulting mixing matrix would be physical only as a relative polar class between two fermionic sectors
      sharing a generation carrier.
    \end{corollary}

    Premises: the generation block, [H-Grad], and the Sylvester equation of Section~\ref{sec:polar}, together with the
    definition of $\Aodd$ and Proposition~\ref{prop:reality-structure}; the real cascade data
    of~\cite{Beau2026prs}; the branch input for the reading of the diagonal split as a
    Lorentz-chirality imbalance, and [H-Res] where the real data are stated as data on $\Epi^{2}|_{\Cgen}$.
    The statement of~\cite{Beau2026ebj} is used under its own hypotheses, and [H-Fac] is needed to read the block class
    as that of the full operator.
    No conclusion on $[\Upi]$ follows from $\Aodd = 0$.

    \begin{remark}[Two transverse blocks]
      \label{rem:two-transverse-blocks}
      The transverse obstruction has two distinct components.
      On the generation triplet $\Cgen = \langle e_{0}, e_{+}, e_{-}\rangle$ the polar tangent transverse to the
      diagonal-rephasing orbit decomposes into the internal block $e_{0} \leftrightarrow e_{\pm}$ and the
      external block $e_{+} \leftrightarrow e_{-}$, the latter denoted $R_{\mathrm{mix}}$.
      For a single metaplectic $\mathfrak{sl}_{2}$ cascade lifted to $\Sym^{2}$, let $L(M)$ be the lift of
      $M = pE + qF + rH \in \mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})$ to $\Cgen$ and let $\JPi z = S\,\overline{z}$, with
      $S = \Sym^{2}(\varepsilon)$, be the antilinear internal parity of Section~6 of~\cite{Beau2026q14}.
      Then $\Pi_{\JPi\text{-odd}}(X) = \tfrac12\,(X - S\,\overline{X}\,S^{-1})$ and the projection of this note is
      defined by
      \[
        \Aodd = \Pi_{\mathrm{antiherm}}\,\Pi_{\JPi\text{-odd}}\big(L(M)\big) .
      \]
      This is a definition of this note; $\Aodd$ is the projection defined here.
      It is not the anomaly density of~\cite{Beau2026q14} and it is not the polar generator $\Opol$ of
      Section~\ref{sec:polar}.
      The selection of the $\JPi$-odd part is that of the oriented sector of Section~6 of~\cite{Beau2026q14}, where the
      $\Jthree$ coefficient of the step generator is the $\JPi$-odd projection, and that of the transported split $u$
      of~\cite{Beau2026prs}, the $\Jthree$ component of $\Pi_{\JPi\text{-odd}}(\Epi^{2})$; both concern real or
      hermitian operators.
      No source selects the $\JPi$-odd part for the generator of $\Upi$, so $\Aodd$ holds by definition and is not
      the polar generator.
      The vanishing of $\Aodd$ is representation-theoretic, not a property of the step: the $\JPi$-odd anti-hermitian
      operators on $\Cgen$ are exactly the spin-zero and spin-two part of $\mathfrak{u}(3)$, while the image of
      $\mathfrak{sl}_{2}$ is spin one, whose anti-hermitian part $L(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ is entirely $\JPi$-even.
      For the cascade step of Corollary~\ref{cor:negative-closure-present-stratum}, whose logarithm has coefficients
      $p = c\,t$, $q = c\,s$, $r = c\,ts/2$ with $c = \theta/\sinh\theta$ and $t, s$ real, the $\JPi$-even internal
      entry is $\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}\,c\,(s - t)$: it vanishes at $t = s$, where $L(M)$ is real symmetric and its whole
      anti-hermitian part is zero, and it is non-zero otherwise.
      The $\JPi$-even anti-hermitian part $\Pi_{\mathrm{antiherm}}\,\Pi_{\JPi\text{-even}}(L(M)) =
      L\big(\tfrac12(M - M^{\dagger})\big)$ is not part of $\Aodd$; its internal entries are
      $\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}(q - \overline{p})$, which do not vanish for real $p \neq q$.
      The statements below concern $\Aodd$ as defined, and are not statements about that even part.
      For every $M \in \mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})$, real or complex, the $\JPi$-odd part of $L(M)$ is hermitian, so
      $\Aodd = 0$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:reality-structure}).
      Neither transverse block is therefore present in $\Aodd$: the external block $e_{+} \leftrightarrow e_{-}$ has
      no matrix element in the image of $\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})$
      (Remark~6.4 of~\cite{Beau2026q14}), and the internal block $e_{0} \leftrightarrow e_{\pm}$ is absent from the
      $\JPi$-odd anti-hermitian part of that image.
      The $\JPi$-even anti-hermitian part of $L(M)$ is not zero: its internal entry
      $\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}(q - \overline{p})$
      is non-zero for real $p \neq q$, and it is the polar generator $\Opol(0)$ of a family with tangent $L(M)$ at the
      identity.
      The image of $\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})$ therefore reaches the internal block through that part, while the
      external block $R_{\mathrm{mix}}$ has no matrix element in the image (Remark~6.4 of~\cite{Beau2026q14};
      Proposition~6.3 (i)--(ii) of~\cite{Beau2026q14} excludes the internal block by hypothesis, for the oriented odd
      lift it considers).
      A $\JPi$-odd anti-hermitian generator, the kind of object onto which $\Aodd$ projects, lies outside the image
      of $\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})$, in the spin-zero and spin-two components of
      $\mathrm{End}(\Sym^{2} V_{\mathrm{gen}})$.
      No source justifies the selection of the $\JPi$-odd part, and $\Aodd$ is not the polar generator; no physical
      exclusion of the internal block is claimed.
      A spin-two extension of the step model, with a generator having a $\JPi$-odd anti-hermitian component, is a
      distinct model: in it the internal block is an allowed element of $\mathfrak{u}(3)$
      (Proposition~\ref{prop:reality-structure}), neither excluded nor constructed, since no source supplies the
      generator.
      The chiral-frontier normalisation channel and the polar-mixing channel separate: the oriented
      area $N_{A}$ belongs to the real Cartan / $\Jthree$ channel~\cite{Beau2026aar} and can normalise the
      diagonal split without predicting a CKM/PMNS polar class.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{proposition}[Reality structure of the $\mathfrak{sl}_{2}$ lift]
      \label{prop:reality-structure}
      Let $M = p\,E + q\,F + r\,H \in \mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})$ with $p, q, r \in \mathbb{C}$, $L(M)$ its lift to
      $\Cgen = \Sym^{2}(V_{\mathrm{gen}})$ in the weight basis $(e_{0}, e_{+}, e_{-})$, $\JPi z = S\,\overline{z}$ the
      antilinear parity with $S = \Sym^{2}(\varepsilon)$, and $\Pi_{\JPi\text{-odd}}(X) =
      \tfrac12(X - S\,\overline{X}\,S^{-1})$, $\Pi_{\JPi\text{-even}}(X) = \tfrac12(X + S\,\overline{X}\,S^{-1})$.
      Then
      \begin{enumerate}
        \item[\emph{(i)}] $S\,\overline{L(M)}\,S^{-1} = -\,L(M)^{\dagger}$;
        \item[\emph{(ii)}] $\Pi_{\JPi\text{-odd}}\big(L(M)\big) = \tfrac12\big(L(M) + L(M)^{\dagger}\big)$ and
          $\Pi_{\JPi\text{-even}}\big(L(M)\big) = \tfrac12\big(L(M) - L(M)^{\dagger}\big)$;
        \item[\emph{(iii)}] $\Aodd = \Pi_{\mathrm{antiherm}}\,\Pi_{\JPi\text{-odd}}\big(L(M)\big) = 0$ for all
          $(p, q, r) \in \mathbb{C}^{3}$;

      \begin{lemma}[Separation of the generation label and the Schur-residue weight]
        \label{lem:separation-label-weight}
        On the common rank-three carrier \(\Cgen = \mathrm{Sym}^{2}(V_{\mathrm{gen}})\) the generation label and the
        Schur-residue weight are distinct, multiplicatively acting factors.
        The label \(g\in\{1,2,3\}\) is fixed by the three admissible stratigraphic levels of the binary
        icosahedral group~\cite{Beau2026a4}.
        The weights \(w_g\in\{1, \tfrac35, \tfrac25\}\) are, under [H-Res] and [H-Sq], the \(J_3\)-triplet of
        \(\Hpi\) at the dictionary gate and are not identified with the stratigraphic level ratios.
      \end{lemma}
      \begin{proof}
        The two factors depend on disjoint data.
        The weight \(w_g\) is a function of the single vertical datum \(u\) alone: the diagonal
        \(\diag(1,\tfrac12+u,\tfrac12-u)\) commutes with \(J_3\) for every \(u\), so the squared-residue split is
        free under \(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}\).
        The stratigraphic factor \(S_g\) is the Kesten--McKay and representation datum \(c_i/\dim\rho_i\) of the
        \(2I\) Cayley spectrum~\cite{Beau2026a5}.
        Their dimensionless ratio-sets differ: \(w_g\) gives \(\{1, \tfrac32, \tfrac52\}\) while the stratigraphic
        levels \(\{20,24,30\}\) give \(\{1, \tfrac65, \tfrac32\}\).
        The ratio sets of \(\sqrt{w_g}\), \(\{1, \sqrt{3/2}, \sqrt{5/2}\}\), the quantities that enter
        Equation~\eqref{eq:mass-factorisation}, are likewise distinct from \(\{1, \tfrac65, \tfrac32\}\).
        Hence the two readings cannot be collapsed into a single observable.
      \end{proof}

      \begin{lemma}[Level-crossing depths cannot carry the hierarchy]
        \label{lem:level-crossing-cancellation}
        In the exponential trajectory-branching regime \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{proj}}(n)\simeq\lambda_2(n) =
        L_0\exp(\beta^{\ast} n)\), suppose the generation depths are the level-crossing depths
        \(n_{\mathrm{proj}}(\lambda) = (1/\beta^{\ast})\log(\lambda/L_0)\) at which each stratigraphic level
        \(\lambda_g\) becomes projectable.
        Then the depth amplification of~\eqref{eq:mass-factorisation} satisfies
        \begin{equation}
          \label{eq:level-crossing-cancellation}
          \frac{A(n_i)}{A(n_j)} \;=\; \exp\!\bigl(\beta^{\ast}(n_i-n_j)\bigr) \;=\; \frac{\lambda_i}{\lambda_j},
        \end{equation}
        independently of \(\beta^{\ast}\).
        On the admissible levels \(\{20,24,30\}\) the amplified ratios are therefore bounded by \(3/2\), and the
        level-crossing depths cannot carry the charged-fermion hierarchy.
      \end{lemma}
      \begin{proof}
        Substituting \(n_{\mathrm{proj}}(\lambda) = (1/\beta^{\ast})\log(\lambda/L_0)\) into
        \(A(n)=\exp(\beta^{\ast} n)\) gives \(A(n_{\mathrm{proj}}(\lambda)) = \lambda/L_0\); the exponent cancels
        and the ratio reduces to \(\lambda_i/\lambda_j\).
        For the three admissible levels this is at most \(30/20=3/2\), an order-one quantity.
      \end{proof}

      The resolution-density route therefore converts the obstruction into a sharper question, which
      an exact audit of the transport then settles:
      the \(\mathrm{Sym}^2\) Schur-residue transport is scale-free (homogeneous of degree one), carrying only
      order-one residue ratios and never an absolute magnitude, while \(|S|\) enters solely as the Laplacian
      normalisation \(L=I-A/|S|\) that defines \(F_{\mathrm{KM}}\).
      Re-inserting \(|S|\) thus undoes the normalisation defining the density and quantises the un-normalised
      count, the route already locked by \(n_g=\Delta I_g/c_g(p)\); the valency is not forced, so this short
      static lever is excluded.

    \paragraph{Decision point.}
      The remaining mass-hierarchy question has been reduced to whether the stabilisation depths \(n_g\) are
      intrinsic dynamical data of the projective cascade, rather than functions of the static ADE,
      Schur-residue, or Kesten--McKay level data.
      All static routes tested so far produce only order-one ratios, or reintroduce the target scale through an
      unforced quantum choice.
      Thus the mass-sector programme reaches a sharp bifurcation:
      either a genuinely intrinsic depth-selection law is derived, or the present stratum must be recorded as
      giving a conditional three-generation factor, as the multiplicity factor $\Cgen$ (on the supplied rule
      $\sigma_{c}(n_{3}) = 3$), and an order-one
      level split under [H-Res] and [H-Sq], but not the charged-fermion hierarchy.

    \paragraph{Dynamic stabilisation status.}
      The remaining dynamic reading of the projective mass cascade does not alter the exclusion
      of the static routes above.
      If the three generation depths are read as parallel thresholds on the same admissibility
      flow, with threshold fractions fixed by the \(J_3=\mathrm{diag}(0,1,-1)\) carrier on
      \(\mathbb{C}^3_{\mathrm{gen}}\simeq\mathrm{Sym}^2(\mathbb{C}^2)\), the resulting depth
      gap is independent of the Kesten--McKay band-edge coefficient.
      In particular, it does not inherit the \(c_1\to 0\) edge divergence or the \(c_2=1/2\)
      symmetry pin that affect all \(c_g\)-routed static depths.
      This is a structural improvement, but not a hierarchical one: the forced gap remains
      on the order-one level-crossing scale.

      The sequential Born--Infeld cycle reading gives the only possible amplification of a
      single intrinsic dynamic number.
      However, every forced dynamic range supplied by the admissibility data remains too small:
      the normalised Heisenberg band, the Alon--Boppana adjacency band, and the generation-level
      span all give cumulative depths far below the charged-fermion hierarchy scale.
      A range large enough to reach the hierarchy would have to be introduced either through
      the cascade amplification itself, which makes the cycle count tautological, or through an
      unpinned ceiling-to-floor scale such as \(\beta_{\mathrm{BI}}/A_{\min}\).
      Both routes reintroduce a free normalisation under another name.

      The renewed-floor multi-cycle escape is therefore also excluded within the family tested at this stratum.
      A transfer law
      \[
        \mathrm{floor}_{g+1}=\mathcal{R}(\mathrm{ceiling}_g)
      \]
      would be admissible only if \(\mathcal{R}\) were already forced by the
      Born--Infeld, ADE, Heisenberg, or Schur structure.
      The available forced renewals give only order-one to order-ten cumulative depths; the
      only renewals reaching the charged-fermion scale are circular.
      Thus the projective cascade gives a conditional three-generation factor (Theorem~5.2 of~\cite{Beau2026q14}, as the
      multiplicity factor $\Cgen$, on the supplied rule $\sigma_{c}(n_{3}) = 3$; its spinor and gauge dressing needs
      [H-Spin] and [H-Weak]),
      the order-one projected
      splitting, and a band-edge-independent order-one depth gap, but it does not derive the
      charged-fermion hierarchy.
      The hierarchy is not carried by any forced static or first-order dynamic quantity of the
      cascade.

    \paragraph{Premises of the level and depth statements.}
      The statements of this section that read the levels $\lamY^{2}\{1, \tfrac35, \tfrac25\}$ as squared Yukawa
      levels, and the Schur-residue weights $w_g$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:separation-label-weight} as the
      $\Jthree$-triplet of $\Hpi$, use [H-Res] and [H-Sq]; the arithmetic of the level ratios does not.
      Lemma~\ref{lem:level-crossing-cancellation} and Corollary~\ref{cor:intrinsic-branching-depths} concern the
      depth amplification of the exponential law and the static spectral data $\{\lambda_g, \dim\rho_g, \beta^{\ast}\}$;
      they use neither the Yukawa morphism, nor the line $\LY$, nor the Lorentz or weak typing, nor
      [H-Res], [H-Sq], [H-Fac], nor the branch input.

    \paragraph{Summary.}
      Front~3b yields the squared Yukawa levels under [H-Res] and [H-Sq], not a mixing result, and Front~3c
      characterises the chiral polar factor of the generation block as a rephasing class whose non-triviality is the
      transverse metaplectic datum.
      Under [H-Res] and [H-Sq], the squared residue $\Epi^{2}|_{\Cgen}$ fixes $\Hpi = \Ygen^{\dagger}\Ygen$; it does not
      fix $\Ygen$ unless the class $[\Upi]$ is fixed.
      The projection $\Aodd$ of the $\mathfrak{sl}_{2}$ lift vanishes by representation theory for every complex
      coefficient, a modelling choice that no source justifies, and it does not determine $[\Upi]$:
      the polar generator of the $\Sym^{2}$ lift is non-zero, and the corpus supplies no family of Yukawa morphisms
      whose polar factor is $\Upi$.

  \section{Reproducibility}
    \label{sec:repro}

    The scripts in \texttt{code/} combine exact arithmetic with explicitly delimited numerical checks.
    The symbolic checks use SymPy; the numerical operations and tolerances are specified below.
    Their printed outputs are committed beside them.
    \begin{itemize}
      \item \texttt{front3b\_yukawa\_operator.py} checks the level substitution and spectrum of
        Proposition~\ref{prop:squared-yukawa} (the spectrum of $\Hpi$ for an explicit non-monomial unitary $\Upi$), the
        polar
        non-uniqueness of Proposition~\ref{prop:polar-ambiguity} (for a generic positive $\Hpi$, a generation rotation
        and
        a diagonal phase leave $\Hpi$ invariant while changing $\Ygen$), the invariance of $\Hpi$ under right-carrier
        unitaries, and the diagonal no-mixing CP-real branch.
        Its spectrum check uses floating-point sorting keys and compares the sorted eigenvalues exactly.
        It does not test [H-Res], [H-Sq] or [H-Fac].
      \item \texttt{front\_polar\_class\_rephasing.py} checks Proposition~\ref{prop:polar-class} in exact arithmetic
        over $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ on a generic and on degenerate $\Hpi$: the $\Jthree$ commutant, the class transformation,
        the
        invariance of the moduli and of $\JCP$, and, in 60-digit arithmetic with a fixed seed, the dimension five of the
        rephasing orbit and the rank four of the differential of the moduli and $\JCP$ at random points of $\UU(3)$.
      \item \texttt{front\_reality\_structure\_sl2\_lift.py} checks Proposition~\ref{prop:reality-structure}: the
      identity
        $S\,\overline{L(M)}\,S^{-1} = -L(M)^{\dagger}$, the vanishing of $\Aodd$ for symbolic complex $(p, q, r)$, the
        six-dimensional $\JPi$-odd anti-hermitian subspace and its orthogonality to $L(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C}))$,
        the
        $\JPi$-even internal entry, a negative control with $\mathrm{antiherm}(L(E)) \neq 0$ while $\Aodd(L(E)) = 0$,
        a negative control with the linear involution $X \mapsto S X S^{-1}$ (which gives
        the condition $\Im(p+q) \neq 0$ and is not the parity of this note), and the leading orders of the detectors of
        Proposition~\ref{prop:polar-nontriviality}.
      \item \texttt{front\_polar\_generator.py} checks, in exact arithmetic, the polar generator of
        Section~\ref{sec:polar}: the Sylvester equation on the family $\Sym^{2}g(\gamma)$,
